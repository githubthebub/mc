# Module 8 — Scenarios & Drills

Knowledge without reps is trivia. This module applies the full system to the negotiations you'll actually face, then gives you a training regimen that turns techniques into reflexes.

---

## 8.1 Salary negotiation (the highest-ROI negotiation of your life)

A $10K increase at 30, compounded through raises calculated off base, is worth hundreds of thousands over a career. Preparation-to-stakes ratio here is absurdly favorable.

**Prepare (Module 6):**
- Market data from 3+ sources (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, recruiters, peers). Your **target**: top-quartile for the role, justifiable. Reservation point from your BATNA — and *build* the BATNA: interview elsewhere first if at all possible.
- Their constraints (Module 5 §5.5): comp bands, budget cycles, the manager's own incentive (they want you happy AND in-band, with a story for HR — build them that story).

**Execute:**
- **Let them anchor if you can** ("What range did you have in mind for the role?"); if forced, use a **bolstering range** (Module 2 §2.9): *"Similar roles at comparable firms are landing $128–142K total."* Bottom of range = your target.
- Voss's salary special: **negotiate the non-salary terms enthusiastically** (scope, title, review timing, learning budget) — and ask: *"What does it take to be successful here?"* Then: *"I'd love your help — how can we make sure I'm part of the projects that matter?"* You've turned your boss into your career agent.
- Raise ask at a current job: accusation audit first (*"You might think this is bad timing, and that I should just be glad about the reorg..."*), then evidence (your written impact log), then a calibrated close: *"How can we get my comp to reflect this scope?"*
- Counter pressure ("we need an answer today") = manufactured scarcity (Module 4 §6): *"If it's a good offer today, it's a good offer Thursday. I want to say yes with full commitment, not under a clock."*
- **Never accept in the room. Never negotiate against yourself** (if they say "what number works?", give your range once — don't lower it because of silence).

## 8.2 Buying a car (the classic adversarial arena)

- **All leverage is built before you arrive:** financing pre-approved, out-the-door quotes emailed from 3+ dealers (make them bid against each other from your inbox — email is your friend: no showroom pre-suasion, no ticking-clock theater), invoice/fair-price data, month-end/quarter-end timing (their quota clock, Module 5 §5.5).
- In the room, the salesperson's tools are pre-suasion (the test drive, "what monthly payment works?") and the 4-square worksheet. Counter: **negotiate only the out-the-door price**, one variable, in writing. Trade-in and financing are separate negotiations *after* the price is locked.
- Ackerman it (Module 6 §6.2): open 65% of your researched target with an accusation audit and a smile; precise final number; non-monetary ask at the end (mats, service credit).
- Their "let me check with my manager" is good-cop/bad-cop + commitment pressure. Your version: *"I have to run the final number past my spouse/my own math overnight"* — the higher-authority defense works both ways.
- Be cheerfully willing to leave. **The walkout is the strongest move in retail negotiation** — and only works if your BATNA (the other dealer's emailed quote) is real.

## 8.3 Buying/renting a home

- Information asymmetry is the game: days-on-market, price-drop history, seller's situation (probate? relocation? already bought?), comparable sales. Your agent can find most of this — **make them** (remember the principal–agent gap, Module 5 §5.5: your agent is paid to close, not to maximize your outcome).
- The listing agent leaks (liking + reciprocity): at the open house ask gently: *"What's the sellers' timeline? Have they found their next place?"* Every answer is leverage.
- Offer with **justified precision** ($412,300 with comps attached beats $410,000 bare) + a cover story the seller can accept without face-loss ("based on the three comparable sales attached...").
- Rentals: landlords price vacancy risk. Offer certainty (longer lease, immaculate references, autopay, flexible start) against rent — that's ZOPA-widening (Module 1 §2) with currencies cheap to you.

## 8.4 Vendor / client contract negotiations

- **Qualify decision process first** (every culture): *"Walk me through how this decision gets made on your side — who's involved, what's the timeline?"* You cannot play the board you can't see.
- Procurement's job is to commoditize you; your job is differentiation + normative leverage: their stated evaluation criteria, their execs' public priorities, their track record of paying for quality.
- The discount ask: never concede naked (Module 1 §6). *"We can get to that number — with annual prepay / two-year term / a case study / reduced scope. Which works?"* Every price move buys something.
- Renewals: your leverage peaks 90+ days out (time to actually switch = credible BATNA). Their "auto-renewal already passed" is a policy, not physics: *"How do we fix this so it works for both of us?"*
- Client pushing scope creep: cheerful reciprocity, every time (see India playbook — but it's universal): *"Absolutely can do — here's the change order."*

## 8.5 Everyday life (where you train for free)

- **Hotel/airline/telecom service recovery:** the agent has discretion + metrics. Liking + calibrated question beats anger every time: *"I know this isn't your fault. What are you able to do in situations like this?"* Then silence. If empty: *"Who would have the authority to help with this?"* — escalate warmly.
- **Bills and fees:** *"I've been a customer for six years. The late fee — is that something you can remove?"* (No-oriented, low-stakes, works shockingly often.) Retention departments have the real discounts; the word is *"I'm considering canceling — what options do I have?"*
- **Disputes with neighbors/family:** pure Modules 2–3. Positions are trenches; interests have exits. Label first, problem-solve second, and remember Ury: the goal is a golden bridge, not a victory — you live next to these people.

## 8.6 The training regimen

**Daily micro-drills (pick one per day, 60 days):**

1. **Mirror day:** in every conversation, respond at least once with a 1–3 word mirror + silence. Watch them elaborate.
2. **Label day:** three labels minimum ("It seems like you've had a long day"). Track hit rate.
3. **No-oriented day:** convert every request to no-oriented form ("Is it a bad time?" / "Would it be crazy to...?").
4. **Silence day:** after every ask, count 5 seconds before speaking again. In every negotiation-adjacent moment, let the other side break silence first.
5. **DJ-voice day:** in one tense moment, deliberately slow down and drop pitch. Note what happens to *their* pace.
6. **Discount day (Voss's classic):** ask for something small and legitimate — a coffee upgrade, a late-checkout, a fee waiver — using one technique. The goal is rejection-comfort, not the discount.
7. **Baseline day:** in one meeting, spend 5 minutes consciously recording someone's baseline, then note one deviation and what topic triggered it.
8. **Prediction day:** before any meeting, write your 3 predictions (Module 5 §5.6). Score them after.

**Weekly:** one real negotiation, however small, using a full One-Sheet (Module 6). Then a written debrief: What did I predict? What surprised me (= the Black Swan I missed)? Which technique did I reach for under pressure, and which did I forget?

**Monthly:** one role-play with a friend instructed to be difficult — hardball tactics, fake deadlines, anger. Pressure-test your accusation audits and your calm. Rotate cultures: run the same scenario "as Germany" then "as Japan" using the playbooks.

**The meta-skill being trained:** staying in deliberate, curious, listening mode while adrenaline argues for fight-or-fold. Every drill above is secretly that drill.
