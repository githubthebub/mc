# Module 6 — The Preparation System

Negotiations are usually won before anyone enters the room. This module is a repeatable ritual: the **One-Sheet** (adapted from Voss's Black Swan Group format, extended with Harvard and prediction elements), the **Ackerman plan**, and pre-mortem drills. For any negotiation that matters, preparation takes 30–90 minutes. Do it in writing, every time — the discipline of writing exposes the wishful thinking.

---

## 6.1 The One-Sheet

Copy this template for every significant negotiation.

```markdown
# NEGOTIATION ONE-SHEET — [deal name] — [date]

## 1. THE GOAL
- Best-case target (specific, ambitious, justifiable): ______
- Reservation point (walk-away, derived from BATNA — set NOW): ______
- My BATNA (one concrete sentence): "If no deal, I will ______"
- Their BATNA (best estimate): ______
- Optimism check: aim high — people who expect more get more,
  but the target must survive the "straight face" test.

## 2. THE SUMMARY
Two or three sentences stating the known facts of the situation so
accurately that the OTHER side would respond "that's right."
(If you can't write this, you don't understand their side yet.)

## 3. LABELS / ACCUSATION AUDIT
Every negative they could think or feel about me and my ask:
- "It seems like you feel ______"
- "You're probably thinking ______"
- "It might look like I'm ______"
(Write at least five. Deliver the worst ones FIRST in the meeting.)

## 4. CALIBRATED QUESTIONS
3-5 how/what questions targeting what I most need to learn:
- "What's the biggest challenge you face with ______?"
- "How does this affect ______?"
- "What happens if this doesn't get resolved?"
Behind-the-table check: "How does the rest of your team see this?"
"Who else weighs in on this decision?"

## 5. INTERESTS MAP (Harvard)
| | Mine | Theirs (hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|
| Stated position | | |
| Underlying interests (why) | | |
| Basic needs at stake (security/recognition/control) | | |
| Non-price currencies I can trade | | |

## 6. STANDARDS & PROOF (normative leverage)
- Objective criteria supporting my number: ______ (comps, market data,
  precedent, their own policies/statements)
- Their likely criteria, and my response: ______

## 7. PREDICTIONS (Module 5 — score these afterward)
- Their opening: ______ (confidence: %)
- Their true reservation point: ______ (confidence: %)
- Their #1 hidden interest / likely Black Swan: ______ (confidence: %)
- Their negotiator type: Analyst / Accommodator / Assertive
- Their clock & audience: what deadline do they face; whom must they
  justify this to; what victory speech do they need?

## 8. NON-CASH VALUE MENU
Things cheap for me to give, valuable for them to get:
1. ______   2. ______   3. ______
Things cheap for THEM to give, valuable for me:
1. ______   2. ______   3. ______

## 9. CULTURE CHECK (if cross-cultural — Module 7)
- Their scales vs. mine (communication, trust, decision, time): ______
- Adjustments: pace, directness, hierarchy protocol, who opens: ______

## 10. THE ACKERMAN PLAN (if price will be bargained)
Target: ______
Offer 1 (65%): ______   Offer 2 (85%): ______
Offer 3 (95%): ______   Final (100%, precise non-round): ______
Non-monetary throw-in for the final offer: ______
```

## 6.2 The Ackerman plan, worked

The Ackerman model is Voss's systematized bargaining sequence — an anchoring + reciprocity + concession-curve machine (see Modules 1, 2, 4 for why each part works). Example: you're buying, market value ~$10,000, your target $8,000.

| Step | Offer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **$5,200 (65%)** | Extreme anchor, delivered with empathy and a reason, after an accusation audit ("you're going to think I'm insulting you..."). Expect the flinch. |
| 2 | **$6,800 (85%)** | Only after they counter and you've used labels + "How am I supposed to do that?" — each move must be *extracted*, not volunteered. |
| 3 | **$7,600 (95%)** | Shrinking increments (Module 1) signal you're near bottom. Keep trading: "If I stretch to this, could you include delivery?" |
| 4 | **$7,865 (100%)** | Precise, non-round final + a non-monetary item ("...and I could really use the old cover that goes with it"). Reads as the true bottom of your math. |

Rules of engagement:
- **Selling? Invert:** open at 135%, retreat 115% → 105% → 100%.
- Between every step: silence, labels, summaries, calibrated questions. The numbers are scaffolding; the empathy does the moving.
- If they open **inside** your plan (better than your step-1 anticipates), recalibrate upward on the spot — never negotiate against yourself by sticking to a script the facts have beaten.

## 6.3 Setting the target: the psychology of aiming

- **Aspiration drives outcome.** Across studies, negotiators with specific, ambitious targets outperform those with "do my best" goals — you concede to your target, so place it high (Shell).
- But **write the reservation point down first**, before ambition inflates it, and never move it mid-conversation. Moving your own walk-away in the room is the cardinal sin; if genuinely new information changes the math, take a break and re-derive it outside the room.
- Gap too small between target and reservation point? You've pre-conceded. Recheck your market data.

## 6.4 The pre-mortem (Malhotra/Bazerman-style discipline)

Spend ten minutes assuming the negotiation **failed** or you got a **bad deal**, and write why:

- "We lost because they had a deadline we didn't know about." → probe for their clock early.
- "I caved when they went silent." → rehearse sitting through silence (it's almost always the Analyst thinking, not anger).
- "Their 'market standard' claim went unchallenged." → pre-load your criteria and comps.
- "I fell in love with the deal." → re-read your BATNA sentence right before walking in.

Also pre-decide your responses to the five most likely hardball moves (fake deadline, good cop/bad cop, low-ball, "that's our policy," take-it-or-leave-it) — scripted counters for each are in the [Quick Reference](cheatsheets/quick-reference.md).

## 6.5 Rehearsal

- **Say your anchor and your accusation audit out loud, three times.** The first time you say a big number must not be in the room — your voice will betray the novelty.
- Role-play the hardest five minutes with a friend playing the other side *meanly*. Voss's finding: pressure-tested scripts survive; untested ones evaporate on contact.
- Prepare your **first 60 seconds** verbatim (tone-setting, accusation audit or agenda frame). Everything else can be improvised off the one-sheet.

## 6.6 Logistics as strategy

- **Venue:** their office = information about them + comfort concession to them; yours = home advantage; neutral/meal = relationship-first cultures' default (Modules 7). Video: cameras on, headline your labels more explicitly — you're down to 7% + 38% (words + tone).
- **Who attends:** match seniority (critical in Japan, Germany, India — Module 7). Never be outnumbered at a decision meeting without a reason.
- **Agenda:** whoever writes the agenda pre-suades (Module 4). Volunteer for the recap email, always — the summarizer's language becomes the record.
- **Energy:** never negotiate anything important tired, hungry, or rushed. Decision fatigue is a measurable, exploitable weakness — and the other side may schedule for it.
